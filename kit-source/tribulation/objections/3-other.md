# Other common objections

### O24. "The 144,000 are Jewish only" (or "they are the saints raised in Matthew 27," or "Rachel's children").
Keywords: 144,000, Jewish only, twelve tribes, firstfruits, Matthew 27:52, Rachel's children
Answer: The 144,000 are sealed on earth, as living servants, just before the harm: "Hurt not the earth... till we have sealed the servants of our God in their foreheads" (Revelation 7:3), and the locusts may hurt "only those men which have not the seal of God in their foreheads" (9:4). People already raised and in heaven wouldn't need that seal. The tribe list itself points beyond ancestry: Dan is missing, Levi (which had no land) is included, and both Joseph and his son Manasseh are named (7:5-8). Paul says who Abraham's seed is: "if ye be Christ's, then are ye Abraham's seed, and heirs according to the promise" (Galatians 3:29). So this research reads the 144,000 as God's sealed servants from every people.
They are "the firstfruits unto God and to the Lamb" (14:4), and firstfruits come right before the harvest: the 144,000 stand with the Lamb (14:1-5), the everlasting gospel goes "to every nation" (14:6), and then "the harvest of the earth is ripe" (14:15). They are described by how they lived: "they which follow the Lamb whithersoever he goeth... in their mouth was found no guile" (14:4-5). Every believer is sealed by the Spirit (Ephesians 1:13); the 144,000, already sealed as believers, are sealed a second time for faithful preaching with no guile and the Spirit's empowerment (14:5), while every believer keeps teaching and witnessing under the great commission (Matthew 28:19-20) until mercy closes. In this research, they carry the testimony through the 1,260 days and are killed when it is finished (11:7); after three days and a half the slain sealed messengers are raised and taken up as firstfruits before the harvest (11:11-12; 14:4). The martyrs of every age under the altar (6:9) wait for these "fellowservants... that should be killed as they were" (Revelation 6:11).

### O25. "God never kills or destroys; Jesus corrected the Old Testament picture of God."
Keywords: God never kills, John 10:10, thief, flood, Jesus corrected the Old Testament
Answer: In John 10:10, Jesus defines the thief in the same chapter: "He that entereth not by the door into the sheepfold, but climbeth up some other way, the same is a thief and a robber" (10:1). He is contrasting Himself with the false shepherd, not saying God never judges. Jesus taught the flood as God's judgment, using the same Greek word for "destroy": "the flood came, and destroyed them all... Even thus shall it be in the day when the Son of man is revealed" (Luke 17:27, 30).
He upheld the Old Testament instead of correcting it: "Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil" (Matthew 5:17), and "the scripture cannot be broken" (John 10:35). He spoke of judgment Himself: "fear him which is able to destroy both soul and body in hell" (Matthew 10:28). And God revealed both sides of His character together: "merciful and gracious, longsuffering... and that will by no means clear the guilty" (Exodus 34:6-7). Judgment came after long mercy: "the longsuffering of God waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was a preparing" (1 Peter 3:20).

### O26. "The two witnesses are Moses and Elijah (or Enoch and Elijah)."
Keywords: two witnesses, Moses and Elijah, Enoch, Revelation 11
Answer: Let John's own clues identify them. "These are the two olive trees, and the two candlesticks standing before the God of the earth" (Revelation 11:4). Zechariah's olive trees feed the lamp with oil, and the angel explains it: "Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith the LORD of hosts" (Zechariah 4:6). Fire comes from their mouth (Revelation 11:5), and God told Jeremiah, "I will make my words in thy mouth fire" (Jeremiah 5:14). Scripture pairs the Spirit and the Word as God's witnesses: "My spirit that is upon thee, and my words which I have put in thy mouth, shall not depart out of thy mouth" (Isaiah 59:21); "the Spirit of truth... he shall testify of me" (John 15:26). They shut heaven like Elijah and turn waters to blood like Moses (11:6): the authority of the Word and the power of the Spirit, as John the Baptist came "in the spirit and power of Elias" (Luke 1:17).
So this research reads the two witnesses as the Holy Spirit and the Word of God, acting together through the sealed 144,000 for 1,260 days, so that "in their mouth was found no guile" (Revelation 14:5). Moses and Elijah show what kind of witness they bear, not who they are.

### O27. "A third temple must be rebuilt in Jerusalem for the Antichrist to defile."
Keywords: third temple, rebuilt temple, temple of God, holy place
Answer: No rebuilt temple is required. The abomination Jesus warned of stood "in the holy place" (Matthew 24:15), which Luke explains as "Jerusalem compassed with armies" (Luke 21:20): Roman armies on the holy ground around the city in AD 70. The second abomination, at day 1,290 (Daniel 12:11), is a death decree against those who refuse the image of the beast (Revelation 13:15), and it needs no building. The man of sin "as God sitteth in the temple of God" (2 Thessalonians 2:4), which Daniel describes as the holy mountain: "he shall plant the tabernacles of his palace between the seas in the glorious holy mountain" (Daniel 11:45). God's people are His temple now: "Know ye not that ye are the temple of God" (1 Corinthians 3:16), and "there is no more offering for sin" (Hebrews 10:18).

### O28. "God has two peoples, Israel and the church, with two separate programs."
Keywords: two peoples, Israel only, dispensationalism, church age, olive tree
Answer: Scripture shows one people of God. Paul pictures one olive tree, with natural branches broken off for unbelief and Gentiles grafted in by faith: "thou, being a wild olive tree, wert graffed in among them, and with them partakest of the root" (Romans 11:17), and "God is able to graff them in again" (11:23). Jesus said, "other sheep I have, which are not of this fold: them also I must bring... and there shall be one fold, and one shepherd" (John 10:16). Paul's mystery is that the Gentiles "should be fellowheirs, and of the same body" (Ephesians 3:6), and Christ "hath made both one" (2:14). "There is neither Jew nor Greek... for ye are all one in Christ Jesus. And if ye be Christ's, then are ye Abraham's seed" (Galatians 3:28-29). One people means one gathering at His coming.

### O29. "Believers are not appointed to wrath, so none of them can die in the fourth seal or the trumpets."
Keywords: fourth seal, trumpets, asteroids, natural disasters, saints die, Christians die, protected, sealed, appointed to wrath, exempt
Answer: Believers are not appointed to wrath, and Paul says in the same breath that they may still die: "For God hath not appointed us to wrath, but to obtain salvation by our Lord Jesus Christ, Who died for us, that, whether we wake or sleep, we should live together with him" (1 Thessalonians 5:9-10). Revelation never calls the fourth seal or the trumpets "wrath." God's wrath "is come" at the seventh trumpet (11:18) and is "filled up" in the seven last plagues (15:1), poured out on "the men which had the mark of the beast" (16:2). Before then the judgments strike "the fourth part" and "the third part" of the earth (6:8; 8:7-12) and call for repentance (9:20), and like every disaster they fall on the just and the unjust (Matthew 5:45; Luke 13:4-5).
The seal of Revelation 7:3 is on "the servants of our God," the 144,000 sealed for their task, and the promise of 9:4 belongs to the fifth trumpet, when the locusts may not hurt anyone with "the seal of God," the 144,000 and every believer sealed by the Holy Spirit: "ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise" (Ephesians 1:13). Egypt shows the same order: the first plagues fell on all, and God severed Goshen from the fourth plague on (Exodus 8:22). So a believer may die in the early calamities, and that death is sleep, not wrath: "Blessed are the dead which die in the Lord from henceforth" (Revelation 14:13).

### O32. "If 'a time, times, and half a time' is 1,260 years in Daniel 7, it must be years in the end-time prophecies too."
Keywords: day for a year, years or days, 1260 years, literal days, time times and half, inconsistent
Answer: Scripture gives the day-for-a-year key for the long prophecies: "each day for a year" (Numbers 14:34); "I have appointed thee each day for a year" (Ezekiel 4:6). That is how the seventy weeks became 490 years and how Daniel 7:25 and Revelation 12:6, 14 became the 1,260 years, 538-1798. In this research, that rule belongs to the time when God's Jubilee calendar was running; it ended at the seventieth Jubilee (1994).
The end-time periods describe the same people living through them from start to finish, which only makes sense as days. The days are shortened so that people survive them: "except those days should be shortened, there should no flesh be saved" (Matthew 24:22). The same men are tormented "five months" (Revelation 9:5). Crowds "see their dead bodies three days and an half" (11:9). And the blessing is for the one who lives to reach the end: "Blessed is he that waiteth, and cometh to the thousand three hundred and five and thirty days" (Daniel 12:12). If these were years, the people described would die long before the periods ended.

### O33. "Is the Pope the Antichrist?"
Keywords: Pope, papacy, Catholic, Antichrist, Rome, little horn, wounded head
Answer: This research does not call any Pope, or any person, the Antichrist. The dragon, "that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan" (Revelation 12:9), is THE Antichrist behind every antichrist power, and "even now are there many antichrists" (1 John 2:18). The man of sin is Lucifer himself, working through the false prophet he possesses, "shewing himself that he is God" (2 Thessalonians 2:4).
History does show the papacy as a system, not a man, in two prophecies: Daniel 7's little horn, which rose as three horns were "plucked up by the roots" (Daniel 7:8) and ruled the 1,260 years (538-1798; 7:25), and the head of the sea beast "as it were wounded to death; and his deadly wound was healed" (Revelation 13:3), one of seven heads. That is this research's reading of history, and it is about a system of power, not the people in it. God has His people in every church and system, and He calls them by name: "Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins" (18:4).

### O34. "The first resurrection of Revelation 20:4 is only for the martyrs."
Keywords: first resurrection, martyrs only, beheaded, Revelation 20:4, partial resurrection
Answer: John names the martyrs first, then those who refused the beast: "I saw the souls of them that were beheaded for the witness of Jesus, and for the word of God, and which had not worshipped the beast, neither his image, neither had received his mark upon their foreheads, or in their hands; and they lived and reigned with Christ a thousand years" (Revelation 20:4). The blessing that follows is for everyone in it: "Blessed and holy is he that hath part in the first resurrection: on such the second death hath no power" (20:6).
Paul gives only one resurrection of the righteous at His coming: "the dead in Christ shall rise first" (1 Thessalonians 4:16), "Christ the firstfruits; afterward they that are Christ's at his coming" (1 Corinthians 15:23). Daniel saw the same two groups as Revelation: "some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt" (Daniel 12:2), with "the rest of the dead" raised after the thousand years (Revelation 20:5). So the first resurrection is for all the righteous, with the martyrs named first for honor.

### O36. "Ezekiel 38-39 is a coming invasion of Israel by Russia (or other nations) before the tribulation."
Keywords: Ezekiel 38, Ezekiel 39, Gog, Magog, Russia, invasion of Israel, Rosh, Meshech, Tubal

They surround "the camp of the saints about, and the beloved city: and fire came down from God out of heaven, and devoured them" (20:9), as Ezekiel saw: "I will rain upon him... great hailstones, fire, and brimstone" (Ezekiel 38:22); "I will send a fire on Magog" (39:6).

### O37. "Revelation 8:2 starts the trumpets right after the seventh seal opens in 8:1, so the trumpets come after the seventh seal."
Keywords: seventh seal, Revelation 8:1, silence in heaven, half an hour, trumpets after the seventh seal, chapter divisions
Answer: The seventh seal opens the book the Lamb received (5:7), and a sealed book can't be read until its last seal breaks. Revelation shows that book opened at the white throne after the thousand years: "another book was opened, which is the book of life" (20:12). Heaven is silent then "that every mouth may be stopped, and all the world may become guilty before God" (Romans 3:19).
Scripture often gives the big picture and then returns for detail: Genesis goes back over creation, "These are the generations of the heavens and of the earth when they were created" (Genesis 2:4); Daniel covers the same kingdoms in chapters 2, 7 and 8; Revelation 12 steps back to Christ's birth. Chapter 7 pauses the seals for the sealing of the 144,000; 8:1 finishes the list of seals in one line; 8:2 begins a new vision, "And I saw the seven angels which stood before God; and to them were given seven trumpets." The trumpets themselves fall within the fourth, fifth and sixth seals (see the timeline). Chapter and verse numbers were added more than a thousand years after John wrote, so the break between 8:1 and 8:2 is not part of the text.

### O38. "The souls under the altar cry out (Revelation 6:9-10), so the dead are conscious."
Keywords: souls under the altar, how long O Lord, conscious dead, immortal soul, Revelation 6:9
Answer: Scripture used this picture before. When Cain killed his brother, God said, "the voice of thy brother's blood crieth unto me from the ground" (Genesis 4:10), and Abel, "being dead yet speaketh" (Hebrews 11:4). The martyrs are seen "under the altar" (Revelation 6:9), where a sacrifice's blood was poured, and their shed blood calls for justice as Abel's did. They are the faithful martyrs of every age, "from the blood of righteous Abel" on (Matthew 23:35).
They are then told to "rest yet for a little season" (6:11), and Revelation explains that rest: "Blessed are the dead which die in the Lord from henceforth: Yea, saith the Spirit, that they may rest from their labours" (14:13). The dead "know not any thing" (Ecclesiastes 9:5); they sleep until "the dead in Christ shall rise first" (1 Thessalonians 4:16). Their question is answered at the third bowl (16:6-7) and fully at His coming.

### O39. "Jesus told the thief, 'To day shalt thou be with me in paradise,' so the saved go to heaven at death."
Keywords: thief on the cross, Luke 23:43, today in paradise, comma, paradise
Answer: The thief asked, "Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom" (Luke 23:42), a day still ahead. Paradise is where God is: "the tree of life, which is in the midst of the paradise of God" (Revelation 2:7). But Jesus did not go there that day. He lay in the tomb, and on Sunday He said, "Touch me not; for I am not yet ascended to my Father" (John 20:17).
Jesus told us when He comes into His kingdom: "When the Son of man shall come in his glory... then shall he sit upon the throne of his glory" (Matthew 25:31). Then "the dead in Christ shall rise first" (1 Thessalonians 4:16), and the thief with them. Since "the dead know not any thing" (Ecclesiastes 9:5), the next thing he sees after closing his eyes is Jesus coming in His kingdom, just as he asked. (The Greek has no punctuation, so "Verily I say unto thee to day," as when Moses said "I have set before thee this day life and good," Deuteronomy 30:15, is also possible; this answer rests on John 20:17, not on the comma.)

### O40. "The rich man and Lazarus (Luke 16) shows the dead are conscious in torment or comfort."
Keywords: rich man and Lazarus, Luke 16, Abraham's bosom, great gulf, torment after death
Answer: It is one of Jesus' teaching stories, in a chapter of them: the lost sheep, the lost coin, the lost son and the unjust steward (Luke 15-16). Some details can't be read literally: the rich man has a tongue though his body "was buried" (16:22), and people in torment talk with people in comfort across a gulf. It teaches two lessons. The verdict is final: "between us and you there is a great gulf fixed" (Luke 16:26). And Scripture is enough: "If they hear not Moses and the prophets, neither will they be persuaded, though one rose from the dead" (16:31).
That came true. Jesus raised a real man named Lazarus (John 11:43-44), and the leaders who rejected Moses and the prophets plotted to kill Jesus and Lazarus too (John 12:10-11). It will come true again: at the end of the thousand years the lost are raised, and even then they march against God's city (Revelation 20:8-9).

### O41. "Revelation 14:11 and 20:10 say 'for ever and ever,' so the wicked are tormented forever."
Keywords: for ever and ever, eternal torment, smoke of their torment, no rest day nor night, Revelation 14:11, Revelation 20:10, hell
Answer: Revelation itself says the wicked are "devoured" by fire from heaven at the end of the thousand years (20:9). The fire serves three purposes. Restitution: "they were judged every man according to their works" (20:13), some with "many stripes," some with "few" (Luke 12:47-48). Penalty: "the wages of sin is death" (Romans 6:23). Purification: "he will make an utter end: affliction shall not rise up the second time" (Nahum 1:9), and then "I saw a new heaven and a new earth" (Revelation 21:1).
Scripture's "for ever" means the result can never be undone. Edom's fire "shall not be quenched night nor day; the smoke thereof shall go up for ever," yet the next verse says "the owl also and the raven shall dwell in it" (Isaiah 34:10-11). Sodom suffered "the vengeance of eternal fire" (Jude 7) and is not burning today. Revelation 14:11 borrows Edom's picture: the smoke rises for ever because the judgment is final.
"They have no rest day nor night, who worship the beast and his image" (14:11) describes, in this research's reading, those who take the mark during the great tribulation: they will have no peace, because the Spirit "will reprove the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment" (John 16:8) until God's offer of salvation ends at the seventh trumpet (Revelation 10:7; 11:15-19). Their choice is already final; the reproof gives them no rest, but they will not turn, and in the end they join the war on the saints.

### O42. "Isaiah 66:24 and Mark 9:44 say 'their worm dieth not,' so the punishment never ends."
Keywords: worm dieth not, fire not quenched, Isaiah 66:24, Mark 9:44, gehenna
Answer: Read what Isaiah's worshippers see: "they shall go forth, and look upon the carcases of the men that have transgressed against me: for their worm shall not die, neither shall their fire be quenched" (Isaiah 66:24). Carcases are dead bodies, not living people in pain. The worm is Scripture's picture of a body consumed in death: "the worm is spread under thee, and the worms cover thee" (Isaiah 14:11); Herod "was eaten of worms, and gave up the ghost" (Acts 12:23). A worm that does not die is one nothing stops until its work is done.
"Not quenched" means no one can put the fire out until it has finished. God said of Jerusalem, "it shall devour the palaces of Jerusalem, and it shall not be quenched" (Jeremiah 17:27); that fire burned in 586 BC and is not burning today. Malachi gives the result: "they shall be ashes under the soles of your feet" (Malachi 4:3).

### O45. "What about people who never heard of Jesus? How can God judge them fairly?"
Keywords: never heard, unreached, heathen, other religions, born in another country, ignorance, without excuse, conscience, Romans 1:20, Romans 2:14-15, fairness of judgment
Answer: God never leaves anyone without a witness. He speaks through creation: "For the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even his eternal power and Godhead; so that they are without excuse" (Romans 1:20); through His kindness: "he left not himself without witness, in that he did good, and gave us rain from heaven, and fruitful seasons" (Acts 14:17); and through Christ Himself: "That was the true Light, which lighteth every man that cometh into the world" (John 1:9).
In this research's reading, every person is measured by the same two witnesses God's law requires (Deuteronomy 19:15): the Holy Spirit, who "will reprove the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment" (John 16:8), and the Word of God. The Word's core, God's law of love (Matthew 22:37-40), is written on every heart: "the work of the law written in their hearts, their conscience also bearing witness" (Romans 2:15). The written Word adds more light, and the gospel goes to every nation before the end (Matthew 24:14). So even a person who never held a Bible has both witnesses.
Each person answers for the light he or she was given: "he that knew not, and did commit things worthy of stripes, shall be beaten with few stripes. For unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required" (Luke 12:48); "If ye were blind, ye should have no sin" (John 9:41). Peter learned it at Cornelius's house: "in every nation he that feareth him, and worketh righteousness, is accepted with him" (Acts 10:35). Salvation is still through Christ alone: "there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved" (Acts 4:12); God judges "the secrets of men by Jesus Christ" (Romans 2:16), and He "is a rewarder of them that diligently seek him" (Hebrews 11:6). No one is without excuse, because everyone had some light and chose what to do with it (Romans 1:21), and the judgment is open, so all will see that God was fair: "just and true are thy ways, thou King of saints" (Revelation 15:3).

### O46. "Why 538? The Goths were still fighting for Italy after that."
Keywords: 538, Goths, Ostrogoths, Vigilius, Silverius, Justinian, little horn, 1260 years start, three horns
Answer: True: the Goths retook Rome briefly in 546 and 550, and their kingdom ended in 553. But Scripture names the event that opens the 1,260 years: the saints "given into his hand" (Daniel 7:25), after three horns were "plucked up by the roots" (Daniel 7:8). In this research's reading of history (outside Scripture), 538 is when the Goths lost their hold on Rome and on its bishop: they lifted the siege of Rome on 12 March 538, and that year Vigilius, seated by the emperor's general and promised the see by Empress Theodora, became the only lawful bishop of Rome after Silverius's death. Justinian's law had already named the bishop of Rome head of all the churches (533-534); now it could be enforced in Rome. The rest of the structure followed in 545 and 554. This research also anchors the period at its firm end: Pius VI taken captive on 20 February 1798, when "they shall take away his dominion" (Daniel 7:26), 1,260 years after 538.
